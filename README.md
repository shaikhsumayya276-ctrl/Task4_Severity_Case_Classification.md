Task 4 – Severity & Case Classification

Task ID: task4
Case Type: Simulated/Fictional Cases for Educational Purpose

Objective

To classify adverse drug reactions based on seriousness and expectedness and evaluate their clinical impact using pharmacovigilance principles.

---

Case 1 – Amoxicillin

ADR: Nausea
Seriousness: Non-serious
Severity: Mild
Expectedness: Expected
Clinical Impact: Low

Justification: The patient experienced mild nausea without hospitalization, disability, life-threatening condition, or other serious outcome.

---

Case 2 – Ibuprofen

ADR: Gastrointestinal bleeding requiring hospitalization
Seriousness: Serious
Severity: Severe
Expectedness: Expected
Clinical Impact: High

Justification: The patient required hospitalization because of significant gastrointestinal bleeding. Therefore, the event meets a seriousness criterion.

---

Case 3 – Paracetamol

ADR: Acute liver injury requiring hospitalization
Seriousness: Serious
Severity: Severe
Expectedness: Unexpected*
Clinical Impact: High

Justification: The patient developed clinically significant liver injury and required hospitalization.

---

Case 4 – Metformin

ADR: Diarrhea
Seriousness: Non-serious
Severity: Mild
Expectedness: Expected
Clinical Impact: Low

Justification: The patient experienced mild diarrhea without hospitalization or another serious outcome.

---

Case 5 – Warfarin

ADR: Intracranial hemorrhage
Seriousness: Serious
Severity: Severe
Expectedness: Unexpected*
Clinical Impact: Critical

Justification: The event required urgent medical management and hospitalization and represented a potentially life-threatening condition.

---

Case 6 – Amlodipine

ADR: Severe ankle edema
Seriousness: Non-serious
Severity: Severe
Expectedness: Expected
Clinical Impact: Moderate

Justification: The edema significantly affected the patient but did not result in hospitalization, life-threatening condition, disability, or another seriousness criterion.

Conclusion

The cases demonstrate classification of ADRs according to seriousness, severity, expectedness, and clinical impact. Seriousness is determined by defined clinical/regulatory criteria, while severity describes the intensity of the reaction.

*Expectedness in these simulated cases is for educational purposes. In an actual pharmacovigilance case, expectedness must be checked against the applicable reference safety information.

Reference
